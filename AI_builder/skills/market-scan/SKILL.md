# Market Scan

TJ is an engineer-entrepreneur who builds businesses out of his own ideas. His top quarterly goal includes finding SIMplicity's marketplace value while still developing it, and his global instructions ask for direct pushback, not validation for its own sake. This skill produces a structured gap/value assessment, not a pitch deck.

## Step 1 — Pin down the idea

State back, in one or two sentences, what the idea actually does and who it's for. If TJ's description is vague on either point, ask before researching — a scan of a fuzzy idea produces fuzzy output.

## Step 2 — Who's already in the space

Search for existing companies, products, or open-source tools solving the same or an adjacent problem. List the real competitors/near-competitors found, not a hedge like "there could be competitors." If nothing turns up after a real search, say so plainly — that's a signal, not a gap in your search.

## Step 3 — Where the gap actually is

Compare what exists against the idea. Be specific about what's missing: a underserved segment, a price point nobody serves, a workflow nobody's automated, a geography, a feature set. If the "gap" is thin or the space is crowded, say that directly — don't manufacture a gap to make the idea sound better. This is the step TJ explicitly wants challenged, not rubber-stamped.

## Step 4 — Rough sizing

Give a back-of-envelope market size estimate with the reasoning shown (who's the buyer, how many of them, what they'd plausibly pay, how often). Label it clearly as a rough estimate, not a number to cite externally. If good sizing data isn't available, say what's missing and what would need to be true for the number to be trustworthy.

## Step 5 — Verdict

End with a direct call: worth pursuing as-is, worth pursuing with a specific pivot, or not a real gap. One paragraph, no hedging, no "it depends" without saying on what.

## Output format

Short and scannable — headers for each step above, no filler intro or closing summary. This is a working document TJ will act on, not a report to present.
